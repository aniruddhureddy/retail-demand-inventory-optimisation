01

Recommended decision

Pilot the scenario-based replenishment policy using actual retailer costs and inventory. In the base case, it reduced simulated operating cost from $2,774 to $1,977 and improved unit fill rate from 95.3% to 97.3%.

The trade-off is material: average inventory value increased from $3,655 to $4,814 (31.7%). The purchasing-budget rule is identical across policies, but actual procurement spend differs. The recommendation depends on whether improved availability justifies the additional working capital.

Across nine combinations of budget and lead time, the scenario policy had lower simulated operating cost than both cover rules. All 108 optimisation runs reached the solver’s optimal status within its specified tolerance, and no purchasing limit was breached. This sensitivity grid uses the same historical period; it is not nine independent business trials.

04

How the project works

  1. Prepare and verify: join daily sales, calendar and weekly prices; reconcile 1,337,156 unit sales. Execute local PySpark windows and Parquet output, with Python and SQL parity checks.
  2. Prevent future-data leakage: training targets end by each origin. Future sales and future prices do not enter predictors; known calendar and event features do.
  3. Compare forecasts: predict all 28 horizons directly. Refit only as time advances, and lock the method using validation.
  4. Connect forecasts to decisions: refresh predictions at weekly reviews, account for stock on hand and in transit, and enforce a shared cash limit.
  5. Evaluate the trade-off: track fulfilled demand, stockouts, inventory value, procurement spending, holding cost and shortage penalties.
05

Limits before a real deployment

  • The cohort favours established, relatively high-volume products in one store. It excludes cold-start and broader store-network behaviour.
  • Historical sales are used as demand. Unobserved historical stockouts may mean true demand was higher.
  • Inventory, costs, lead times and penalties are assumed. Higher holding-cost or lower stockout-penalty assumptions can change the decision.
  • The optimisation chooses from a finite set of order quantities under a simplified protection-period objective. It is not a globally optimal dynamic inventory policy.
  • Initial stock changes with lead time, so cross-lead-time results do not isolate delivery-speed effects. Compare policies within scenarios.
  • Prediction intervals undercovered on test data, and only three test windows are available. Further calibration and prospective testing are needed.
